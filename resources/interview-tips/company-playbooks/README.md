# Company Interview Playbooks

This directory contains unofficial, public-source preparation guides for various technology companies. 

**Important Caveat:** The unofficial leveling context, architectural priorities, and any self-review matrices discussed in these guides are derived entirely from public engineering blogs, conference talks, and published system architectures, or unconfirmed historical claims from public forums. They serve strictly as candidate preparation guidance and **do not represent official company rubrics or claims of official status**. Hiring outcomes and level placements are never guaranteed based on this material.

## Index of Playbooks

* **[Amazon SDE II/III Interview Playbook](amazon.md)**
  Explores Amazon's intense focus on their 16 Leadership Principles, unofficial leveling context for SDE II and III roles, core architectural patterns (like DynamoDB and SQS/SNS), and the critical role of the Bar Raiser.

* **[Apple ICT4/ICT5 Interview Playbook](apple.md)**
  Provides unofficial context for Apple ICT4 and ICT5 roles, focusing on Apple's engineering culture prioritizing hardware-software integration, product quality, and a foundational "Privacy-by-Design" constraint.

* **[Google L5/L6 Interview Playbook](google.md)**
  Outlines unofficial public architecture context for Google L5/L6 software engineering interviews. It covers planetary-scale design reasoning, algorithmic clean code, Spanner, Borg, and API design clarity.

* **[Meta E5/E6 Interview Playbook](meta.md)**
  An unofficial preparation guide for Meta E5/E6 interviews focusing on the "bottom-up" engineering culture, TAO cache architecture, Thrift RPC, RocksDB, and Memcached.

* **[Microsoft L63-L67 Interview Playbook](microsoft.md)**
  Explores unofficial enterprise system design context commonly discussed in Microsoft software engineering interviews, centered around the Azure ecosystem, Cosmos DB multi-model options, and enterprise reliability.

* **[Netflix Senior/Staff Interview Playbook](netflix.md)**
  Details unofficial context on Netflix's distinctive "Freedom and Responsibility" culture, focusing heavily on high-concurrency video delivery, chaos engineering, microservice resilience, and architectural patterns using Spring Cloud and Envoy.

* **[Uber L5/L6 Interview Playbook](uber.md)**
  Explores the unique challenges of Uber's real-time planetary scale business. Covers unofficial L5/L6 leveling context, the H3 geospatial index, Ringpop for application sharding, and using Kafka for massive data pipelines.
