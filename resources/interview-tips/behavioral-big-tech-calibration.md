# 🏢 Behavioral Big-Tech Calibration

How to translate your experience into signals expected in structured large-company interview loops.

---

## Overview

Behavioral expectations in large organizations are typically rubric-driven and level-calibrated. The goal is not to mimic company jargon, but to map your decisions to scale, risk, ownership, and collaboration at the target level.

---

## Requirements and constraints

- Show scope in terms of systems, teams, and business impact.
- Balance ownership and collaboration; avoid both "I did everything" and "we did everything".
- Map stories to explicit values/signals without reciting slogans.
- Demonstrate decision quality under constraints (time, reliability, stakeholders).
- Keep examples factual and defensible under deep follow-ups.

---

## Scoring rubric

| Dimension | 1 - Weak | 3 - Solid | 5 - Strong Signal |
|------|------|------|------|
| Scale translation | Generic claims | Some scale framing | Clear scale/risk context with decisions |
| Level calibration | Scope mismatch | Mostly aligned | Crisp alignment with target level expectations |
| Cross-functional signal | Minimal partner context | Some partner interaction | Strong influence across functions and boundaries |
| Values mapping | Slogans | Basic mapping | Evidence-first mapping to behavior and outcomes |
| Credibility | Inflated scope | Plausible narrative | Follow-up resilient and internally consistent |

---

## Common pitfalls

- Replacing evidence with buzzwords.
- Inflating scope beyond what follow-ups can support.
- Ignoring execution mechanics after strategic statements.
- Presenting collaboration as passive participation.
- Using one story for all prompts without re-framing.

---

## Follow-up questions

- What made this a high-scope problem for your level?
- How did you influence teams you did not manage?
- Which trade-off did you intentionally accept?
- What risk did you optimize for, and why?
- How would this approach change at 10x scale?

---

## Variants and extensions

- Startup background variant: emphasize operating discipline and reliability trade-offs.
- Traditional enterprise variant: emphasize speed/ambiguity adaptation with controls.
- Senior IC variant: architecture leadership and technical standards.
- Manager variant: org design, delegation model, and execution predictability.
