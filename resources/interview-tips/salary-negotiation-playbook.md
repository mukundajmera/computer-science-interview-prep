# 💬 Salary Negotiation Playbook

A practical framework for handling offer-stage compensation conversations with confidence, clarity, and low execution risk.

---

## Overview

Salary negotiation is an interview extension, not a separate game. You are still being evaluated on judgment, communication quality, and consistency under pressure.

The strongest outcomes usually come from:
- clear stakeholder routing,
- total-comp thinking instead of base-only focus,
- data-backed asks,
- consistent narrative across calls and emails,
- disciplined closing behavior.

---

## ⏱️ Offer-Stage Timebox

| Phase | Timebox | Objective |
|------|------|------|
| 1. Pre-call preparation | 20-30 min | Define target range, walk-away line, and supporting evidence |
| 2. Verbal offer call | 15-25 min | Understand package details, avoid instant acceptance, set follow-up |
| 3. Counter proposal | 20-40 min | Present a structured ask with rationale and component priorities |
| 4. Counter loop(s) | 1-5 business days | Evaluate movement, protect narrative consistency, reduce risk |
| 5. Written close | 15-20 min | Confirm revised terms and timeline before final acceptance |
| 6. Transition | 10-15 min | Close professionally and protect long-term relationship |

---

## Requirements and constraints

- Keep all compensation negotiation through the recruiter unless explicitly directed otherwise.
- Use one consistent storyline across conversations.
- Optimize for total compensation and role trajectory, not one headline number.
- Stay factual; avoid inflated claims or unverifiable assertions.
- Do not seek legal advice in interview prep docs; verify local regulations independently.
- Treat "best and final" statements as high-signal boundaries and respond professionally.

---

## Stakeholder map

### Hiring manager

**Motivation:** close headcount with high-confidence fit.  
**Discuss:** role scope, team priorities, impact expectations, onboarding goals.  
**Avoid:** direct compensation bargaining unless the recruiter explicitly routes you there.

### Recruiter

**Motivation:** close efficiently while balancing internal policy and candidate experience.  
**Discuss:** package details, priorities by component, market context, timeline, constraints.  
**Avoid:** contradictory narratives across rounds.

### Finance / compensation committee

**Motivation:** maintain equity, budget discipline, and policy consistency.  
**Discuss via recruiter:** structured rationale, comparable offers, component trade-offs, risk of non-close.  
**Avoid:** emotional framing without data.

## Compensation component strategy

| Component | Typical flexibility | Why it moves this way | Candidate strategy |
|------|------|------|------|
| Base salary | Low-Medium | Recurring cost anchor for future raises/bonuses | Ask with evidence; avoid anchoring entire negotiation on base |
| Equity | Medium-High | Forward-looking cost and vesting risk structure | Use impact horizon and retention logic; request grant/refresh clarity |
| Sign-on bonus | High | One-time cost, often used to bridge gaps | Use to offset transition cost, foregone bonus, or vesting loss |
| Performance bonus target | Low-Medium | Often level/job-family bounded | Clarify target %, trigger rules, and payout mechanics |
| Joining timeline flexibility | Medium | Hiring velocity and team needs vary | Trade timing flexibility for comp movement when sensible |

**Negotiation heuristic:** define primary lever, secondary lever, and fallback lever before the first counter.

---

## Negotiation execution flow

1. Confirm exact package structure and level assumptions.  
2. Ask for decision window and next-step timeline.  
3. Build one coherent, data-backed counter narrative.  
4. Present component-priority order, not an unstructured list of asks.  
5. Handle responses with calm iteration, not reactive escalation.  
6. Validate final numbers in writing before acceptance.

**Suggested narrative skeleton:**
- Appreciation and role intent
- Why current package is close but not yet aligned
- Component-prioritized ask
- Supporting rationale and constraints
- Clear closing intent if aligned

## Global track

### Baseline guidance

- Use location-adjusted market benchmarking for role/level.
- Distinguish fixed pay, variable pay, and equity risk profile.
- Clarify any relocation, tax, or visa-driven constraints before final acceptance.
- Keep written summaries after each negotiation step.

### Risk controls

- Do not fabricate competing offers.
- Do not force hard ultimatums early.
- Avoid sharing confidential details from current employer docs.
- Verify labor-law and disclosure boundaries in your own jurisdiction.

---

## India track

### India-specific calibration points

- Evaluate package mix across fixed cash, variable pay, joining bonus, retention bonus, and ESOP/RSU terms.
- Account for annual cycle effects (bonus cutoff windows, appraisal timing, notice-period complexity).
- Ask explicit questions on in-hand implications, variable payout criteria, and vesting/tax treatment.
- If relevant, include buyout/notice support and relocation assistance in component strategy.

### India-specific risk controls

- Confirm what evidence is appropriate to share for competing opportunities.
- Avoid legal interpretations in interview prep; verify with official policy and local regulation.
- Document all negotiated terms in final written offer and annexures.

## Scoring rubric

| Dimension | 1 - Weak | 3 - Solid | 5 - Strong Signal |
|------|------|------|------|
| Preparation quality | No target or evidence | Basic target and rationale | Clear ranges, priorities, and fallback plan |
| Stakeholder routing | Wrong channel usage | Mostly correct routing | Precise routing with relationship protection |
| Ask quality | Unstructured ask | Reasonable ask | Component-prioritized, evidence-backed ask |
| Narrative consistency | Contradictory claims | Mostly consistent | Fully consistent across all interactions |
| Trade-off handling | Rigid position | Some flexibility | Smart lever swaps and pragmatic sequencing |
| Closing discipline | Premature accept/escalate | Adequate closure | Professional close with written confirmation |

---

## Common pitfalls

- Accepting immediately without evaluating component flexibility.
- Negotiating directly with hiring manager when recruiter channel exists.
- Switching rationale between rounds and damaging trust.
- Over-focusing on base salary while ignoring easier levers.
- Pushing beyond communicated final boundary without strategy.
- Framing negotiation as adversarial rather than collaborative.

---

## Follow-up questions

- Which component should be primary vs fallback for this offer mix?
- How should you respond when movement is offered only in non-base levers?
- What is the safest response to "best and final"?
- Which details must be confirmed in writing before acceptance?
- How should timeline pressure change your strategy?

---

## Variants and extensions

- **No competing offer variant:** maximize via component trade-offs + timeline flexibility.
- **Competing offer variant:** use comparative framing without disclosing unnecessary confidential details.
- **Internal move / retention variant:** apply the same rubric, with added role-scope clarity.
- **Manager-round adaptation:** emphasize team impact, scope, and long-term commitment signals.

---

## Additional notes

This guide is interview-prep oriented. It is not legal, tax, or financial advice. For jurisdiction-specific compliance questions, verify with official sources and qualified professionals.
