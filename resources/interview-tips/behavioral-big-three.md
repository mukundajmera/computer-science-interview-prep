# 🎯 Behavioral Big Three

A focused preparation guide for the three prompts that appear in most loops:
- Tell me about yourself
- Tell me about your favorite project
- Tell me about a conflict you resolved

---

## Overview

The big three are high-leverage because they establish your level signal early and drive follow-up depth. Strong responses are concise, evidence-backed, and adaptable by audience (IC and manager).

---

## Requirements and constraints

- Keep "tell me about yourself" to 60-90 seconds.
- Keep flagship project and conflict stories to 2-3 minutes, then handle follow-ups.
- Use explicit ownership language: what you personally decided and drove.
- Include measurable or operational outcomes.
- Preserve confidentiality while keeping decisions concrete.
- Maintain one base story and adapt emphasis per signal area.

---

## Scoring rubric

| Dimension | 1 - Weak | 3 - Solid | 5 - Strong Signal |
|------|------|------|------|
| Story selection | Low-scope mismatch | Relevant, acceptable scope | High-scope, high-fit, level-appropriate |
| Ownership clarity | Mostly "we" | Mixed ownership language | Clear personal decisions/actions |
| Trade-off quality | No alternatives discussed | Basic comparison | Explicit alternatives + rationale |
| Impact evidence | Vague outcome | Some concrete outcomes | Multi-dimensional measurable impact |
| Reflection | Generic lesson | Specific but shallow | Behavioral change with repeatable principle |

---

## Common pitfalls

- Chronological biography for TMAY instead of role-fit summary.
- Favorite project story without constraints or trade-offs.
- Conflict story framed as personal victory instead of durable resolution.
- Overlong context and low action density.
- Metrics omitted when they are available.

---

## Follow-up questions

- Why did you choose this path over alternatives?
- What was your exact ownership boundary?
- What changed for users, business, and team operations?
- What would you do differently now?
- How did the relationship with stakeholders evolve after the conflict?

---

## Variants and extensions

- IC variant: emphasize technical decisions, incident/risk handling, cross-team influence.
- Manager variant: emphasize prioritization, delegation, coaching, stakeholder alignment.
- Senior variant: show org-level trade-offs and second-order effects.
- Practice extension: answer each of the big three with the same story and different signal emphasis.
