# ⚠️ Behavioral Interview Pitfalls

High-frequency mistakes in behavioral interviews and concrete recovery patterns.

---

## Overview

Most misses come from execution, not lack of experience: wrong story selection, weak ownership clarity, low action density, and poor follow-up handling.

---

## Requirements and constraints

- Every answer must show signal alignment, not just storytelling quality.
- Use concise context and action-first delivery.
- Preserve factual accuracy under deep follow-ups.
- Show both outcome and learning.
- Keep level calibration explicit for IC and manager tracks.

---

## Scoring rubric

| Dimension | 1 - Weak | 3 - Solid | 5 - Strong Signal |
|------|------|------|------|
| Signal targeting | Off-target answer | Partial match | Explicitly aligned and sustained |
| Action density | Mostly background | Balanced context/actions | Decision-heavy, high information density |
| Ownership clarity | Blurry ownership | Some clarity | Crisp ownership boundaries and decisions |
| Credibility | Inconsistent details | Mostly consistent | Follow-up resilient and defensible |
| Growth signal | No reflection | One lesson | Behavior change + operating mechanism |

---

## Common pitfalls

### 1) Missing the underlying assessment
Answer sounds polished but addresses the wrong signal.

### 2) Context overload
Too much setup leaves little room for decisions and outcomes.

### 3) "We" disease
Team activity is described, personal contribution is unclear.

### 4) Wrong story for level
Story scope is too small for the target role.

### 5) Fairy-tale endings
No setbacks or trade-offs, reducing credibility.

### 6) Weak follow-up handling
Answer changes shape under probing and becomes inconsistent.

### 7) Reflection without behavior change
Lesson is generic and not connected to repeatable practice.

### 8) Senior/manager pitfall: strategy without operations
High-level statements lack execution mechanics and metrics.

---

## Follow-up questions

- What exactly did you decide and own?
- Which alternative did you reject, and why?
- What failed first and how did you respond?
- How do you know your change worked?
- What did you institutionalize afterward?

---

## Variants and extensions

- IC recovery drill: rewrite one weak story with explicit decision timeline.
- Manager recovery drill: add delegation model and execution controls to each leadership story.
- Senior recovery drill: add risk framing and second-order impact in every close.
- Peer-review extension: score only pitfalls and recovery quality.
